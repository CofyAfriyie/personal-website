        Methods

        No one predicts a single share reliably

        In well-known studies and university notes, history-based predictors rarely beat a plain average on new data. Good practice shows a range, not one number.

        GSE back-test

        Past returns did not predict the next ones

        As if at each year-end since 2018: carrying recent returns forward did no better than assuming "no change". One-year outcomes ran from about half to double.

        News and events

        No type of news reliably moved prices

        Against comparison dates, no category of announcement stood out, and many were followed by no trade at all. MTN Ghana's 2018 IPO raised about a third of the shares offered.

        Treasury bills

        "Today's rate holds" was hard to beat

        For 91 to 364-day bills, assuming the latest rate persists beat every alternative tried. Rolled bills still swung, and after inflation sometimes lost value.

        Methodology

        How the figures on this page are produced, and what they cannot tell you.

        Where the data comes from

        Share prices, volumes and the indices come from the Ghana Stock Exchange's daily trading data and daily market summary. Treasury-bill rates come from Bank of Ghana auction results. Each figure on this page shows its date; company names come from the companies' own filings and GSE notices.

        "Close" is the exchange's published closing price. Price changes compare closing prices only: dividends and rights are not added, so they understate what a long-term holder received.

        How bad prices are handled

        One- to three-day price spikes that immediately reverse are treated as data errors and dropped, never replaced with a guess. A ticker that was split across two spellings is joined. A sudden, lasting jump of almost exactly 2, 3, 5 or more times is treated as a possible unadjusted share split, and no price change is measured across it. Delisted shares are left out.

        How methods are tested (walk-forward)

        Each method is tested as if it were a past date: only information published by then is used, a forecast is made, and it is compared with what actually happened. This is repeated at every year-end since 2018 and scored against simple benchmarks: "no change", the market average and the treasury-bill rate. A method that cannot beat them is not used.

        Three layers: history, assumption, range

        History is shown as a record and never stretched forward as if it will repeat. Any planning assumption starts from the market's own long-run average, today's treasury-bill rate and a share's own history pulled toward the market. The range is how far outcomes actually strayed from forecasts like this in the back-test: about 8 in 10 outcomes fell inside these bands.

        AfterLowHigh

        For example, GHS 100 with a planning assumption of "no change" could plausibly end anywhere from about GHS 51 to GHS 194 after one year. Beyond 5 years the bands are an extrapolation.

        Limits

        About eight years of data and roughly forty listed shares are enough to reject weak methods but not to prove a strong one. Many shares rarely trade, so a price can stay unchanged for weeks without any trading. Figures are nominal (before inflation), before tax and fees. Nothing here is a forecast, a promise or advice.
