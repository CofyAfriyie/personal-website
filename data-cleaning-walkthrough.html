Cleaning a real accounting ledger

A public general ledger of 21,551 lines looks broken when you open it naively. Step through the cleaning: see what the raw file seems to say, what each step changes, and what still needs research.

Afriyie Kofi Owusu-Ansah6 min readData cleaning

Source and licence: Anonymized General Ledger Dataset by Charm Myae Aung on Kaggle (file GL_Dataset.csv, CC0: Public Domain). The author states it is de-identified and does not represent actual accounting records. Retrieved 8 October 2026. The rows in the table are real rows from that file; the counts and totals are my calculations over all 21,551 lines. The dataset gives no currency and no account names.

The data at this step

What the cleaned data says

Period

1 to 31 May 2026, plus a single line dated 1 June 2026 (a credit of 210 on JR00041).

Balance

Debits 13,863,160,429 against credits 13,863,160,344. The ledger balances to within 85, about six millionths of the total.

Lines

11,421 debit lines and 10,130 credit lines across 102 account codes.

Shape

Posting is uneven: 31 May alone holds 6,960 lines, 32% of the month.

Where further research is needed

  • The 31 May spike. Is it a normal month-end batch or a bulk upload? The file cannot say.
  • The 403 unbalanced IDs. 401 are out by 1 or 2, one by 18 and one by 210. Some IDs look like batches rather than single entries, so the source's posting rules are needed.
  • The 1 June line. It is the only line outside May, and it is exactly the 210 by which JR00041 fails to balance. It may be a cut-off error or a deliberate posting.
  • Account meaning. With no chart of accounts, I cannot say which codes are assets, income or costs. Any statement-level conclusion needs that mapping.
  • Anonymisation effects. The author says codes, dates and amounts were transformed, so the totals suit practising technique, not judging a business.

The broader lesson

The first reading was wrong in several ways, and the most tempting fix (deleting repeated lines) would have broken a ledger that already balanced. Test a cleaning rule against a control total before you apply it.
